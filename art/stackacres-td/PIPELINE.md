# How StackAcres pixel art is made

Everything visual in the top-down rewrite is generated by scripts and rebuilt with one
command, then checked against what Kayo already approved. Nothing is hand-painted in a
way that can't be regenerated. Written 2026-09-16.

```
art/stackacres-td/            (in the repo; generated output is gitignored)
  HANDOFF.md            start here
  PIPELINE.md           this file
  AREAS.md              every area, its gate, and what opens it
  ray-walk.gif          Ray's FIRST approved walk (32x32). The regression reference.
  characters/
    rig/                rig.py, build_aseprite.lua, build.sh, check.py, build/ (generated)
    ray/ farmer/        <name>.aseprite, <name>-sheet.png/.json, previews/*.gif, <name>-contact.png
  areas/
    rig/                kit.py (terrain + the Homestead's sprites), area.py (the Area class),
                        props.py (places, gates, water, smoke), creatures.py (animals), crops.py,
                        people.py (the cast list), sheet.py (contact sheets), build_all.py, and
                        one script per area: homestead.py oldfields.py fold.py pasture.py
                        coast.py oak.py mine.py townsquare.py
    tileset.png         shared 16x16 terrain tileset
    <area>/             <area>.png, <area>-2x.png, views/*.png and views/*.gif (animated), per area
    sheets/             props.png creatures.png crops.png cast.png
  characters/
    rig/wardrobe.py     heads, outfits and palettes for the 12 people who are not Ray or the farmer
    <name>/             14 characters now: ray farmer pierre miles skye barnaby arthur brayden ivy
                        wes bea leo pilgrim merchant
```

## Tools

- **Python 3 + Pillow.** All art is generated.
- **Aseprite v1.3.18.5, built from source** at `~/deps/aseprite/build/bin/aseprite`
  (symlinked as `~/.local/bin/aseprite`) against the Aseprite team's prebuilt Skia m124.
  Rebuild or upgrade with `~/deps/build-aseprite.sh`. Installing packages needs sudo, which
  needs Kayo's password, so Kayo runs `dnf`.
- **pixel-mcp** (Claude Code plugin) drives Aseprite. Config: `~/.config/pixel-mcp/config.json`.
  After changing it, reconnect with `/mcp`. Its **`link_cel` is broken**: it reports success
  but copies instead of linking. Real links come from `app.command.LinkCels()` in Aseprite
  Lua (see `characters/rig/build_aseprite.lua`).
  For bulk art (96 frames) drive Aseprite from a Lua script, not hundreds of MCP calls.
- **LibreSprite can't replace Aseprite for pixel-mcp**: pixel-mcp writes Aseprite Lua, and
  LibreSprite scripts in JavaScript. Fine as a manual editor.

## The style contract (Kayo approved all of this)

- **Palette: DawnBringer 16 only**, everywhere, characters and areas alike. The scripts assert
  no off-palette pixels.
  `K #140C1C  P #442434  B #30346D  g #4E4A4E  N #854C30  G #346524  R #D04648  O #757161`
  `L #597DCE  o #D27D2C  s #8595A1  v #6DAA2C  T #D2AA99  C #6DC2CA  Y #DAD45E  W #DEEED6`
  The single letters are the keys used in every grid in both rigs.
- **Light from the top-left.** Lit edges on the left and top, shade on the right and bottom.
- **Outlines:** a black `K` silhouette on characters and objects. Where an arm crosses the
  body the dividing edge is the shirt's shadow color, not black; tools crossing the body get
  black. No outline on reeds, flowers, lily pads, or terrain.
- **Cluster shading, never per-pixel noise.** Repeating detail (grass tufts, pebbles, ripples)
  comes in hashed variants so it never forms a visible grid.
- **Sizes:** 16×16 tiles. Character frames 48×48, feet at y=44, centered on x=23.5, a body
  about 16 wide × 27 tall. The in-game phone camera is 4× integer scale, about 13×8 tiles.
  Integer scaling only.
- **Simple animation:** 4 frames per animation. Kayo explicitly wants depth from mechanics,
  not animation polish.

## Characters: `characters/rig/`

**Rebuild everything:** `art/stackacres-td/characters/rig/build.sh`
It runs `rig.py` (frames to `build/<name>.json`, `build/<name>-expected.png`, previews, contact
sheets), then `build_aseprite.lua` per character, then `check.py`.

**What each character gets:** 6 animations × 4 directions × 4 frames = 96 frames, tagged
`<action>_<dir>`. Actions: `walk harvest water chop fish shoot`. Directions:
`down up left right`. **Left is an exact mirror of right**, baked into the sheet. Durations
vary per frame and are in the JSON.

**Layers, bottom to top:** `FX Back, Tool Back, Arm Back, Legs, Body, Head, Arms, Tool, FX, Outline`.
FX layers are never outlined. A tool or arm that should sit behind the body facing up goes
on the `Back` layers.

**How rig.py is built:**
- `CHARACTERS`: a character is `{"head": <style>, "roles": {...}}`. Body grids use role keys
  (`S s h` skin, `H j` hair, `C c k` hat, `E` eyes, `R r` shirt and its shadow/plaid,
  `L l Y` overalls, shadow and buckle, `u` cuffs, `D b d` boots) mapped to DB16 keys.
  **To add a character**, add a roles dict. Only add a `HEADS` entry if the silhouette
  differs: 14 wide, `front`/`back`/`side` grids drawn at x=17 from `head_top`, with the
  torso starting 11 rows below `head_top`.
- `TORSO[front|back|side]`, `ARM_L/ARM_R` (walk arms), `front_legs`, and `side_legs` with
  named stances (`together passing near_forward far_forward crouch lunge`).
- `posed(ch, direction, duration, bob, legs, arms, extras)` builds one action frame. The
  `arms` entries are `(layer, "L"|"R"|"near"|"far", hand_xy)`: the shoulder comes from
  `SHOULDER` plus bob, and the hand is an absolute pixel. `extras` draw tools and FX
  (`axe can drops chips rod bow_side bow_front arrow carrot`).
- **To add an animation**, write `name(ch, d)` returning 4 frames for `down`, `up` and
  `right`, then add it to `ANIMATIONS`. Left is generated.
- `Frame.finish()` does the silhouette outline and the arm/tool separation edges.

**Pose rules learned the hard way:**
- Keep shoulder-to-hand at about 9px, the walking arm's length. Longer arms read as sashes,
  or as a box around the head.
- Raised arms go beside the head (front/back) or behind the back of the head (side), never
  across the face.
- Facing up, anything aimed "forward" is behind the body. The bow facing up sits at chest
  height behind the back with only its tips showing. Above the head it looked like a halo.

**Checks (`check.py`), all must pass:**
- Ray's `walk_down` must equal `art/stackacres-td/ray-walk.gif` offset by (+8, +14).
  That was the first thing Kayo approved.
- Every frame of Aseprite's exported sheet equals rig.py's composite, with the same
  durations and tags.
- `build_aseprite.lua` reopens the saved file and prints the shared-cel count, which must
  equal the links it made.
- Review crops per animation land in `rig/build/review/`. **Look at them.** Every real bug so
  far was caught by looking, not by a check.

**Using the sheets in Phaser 3.90:** `<name>-sheet.json` is Aseprite's JSON (array format)
with `frameTags`, so `this.load.aseprite(key, png, json)` plus
`this.anims.createFromAseprite(key)` should create `walk_down` and the rest directly. Verify
this when wiring it up; it hasn't been tried in the game yet.

## Areas: `areas/rig/`

**Rebuild everything:** `python3 art/stackacres-td/areas/rig/build_all.py`, then `python3 art/stackacres-td/areas/rig/export.py` for the game
It renders all eight areas (`<area>/<area>.png`, `-2x.png`, `views/*.png` at 4×), the shared
`areas/tileset.png`, and the contact sheets in `areas/sheets/`. One area alone:
`python3 <area>.py`. Sheets alone: `python3 sheet.py [props|creatures|people|lineups]`.

**`kit.py`, terrain and the Homestead's sprites:**
- **Terrain is corner-based autotiling.** Grass is the base; the overlays, in paint order,
  are `sand gravel mud cobble path soil water` (later ones cut into earlier ones, so the sea
  cuts into the beach and a road crosses the cobbles). Each overlay tile is picked by its 4
  corner bits (15 shapes) using a bilinear coverage function with a small periodic wobble
  (`WOB`, period 16), so edges line up across seams. Fully covered tiles pick a hashed
  interior variant so pebbles, shells and ripples never form a grid.
- **The rim rule.** The one-pixel lip outside an overlay's edge is coloured by what it is
  cut into (`RIM`: dark green on grass, damp brown on sand, dark grey on cobbles, and so on).
  `render_terrain` tracks an owner per pixel to do it. `tileset_image()` still bakes every
  overlay onto plain grass, so a Phaser export needs one rim variant per base.
- Materials are authored on the **vertex grid** ((W+1)×(H+1)). A span of vertices
  `x0..x1` renders from `(x0-0.5)` to `(x1+0.5)` tiles, which is a half-tile offset. Plan
  object positions in pixels with that in mind.
- Grass tiles are motif placements (`GRASS_TILES`) chosen by hash. Sand is `Y` with `W`
  glare and the odd shell; cobbles are 4×4 stones in running bond, hashed for dark stones and
  rare glints; mud is `N` with hashed clods; gravel is `O` with grit. Puddles are NOT in the
  mud material (per-tile puddles formed polka dots); they are `props.puddle`, placed by hand.
- **Sprites** return `(Sprite, anchor)`, where the anchor is the base point in sprite pixels.
  `cast_shadow` darkens the ground through the `SHADE` color map. No alpha.

**`area.py`, the `Area` class every area script uses:**
- `Area(name, w, h)`; terrain with `rect / line (2 wide) / ellipse / clear(material, ...)`.
- `add(made, base_x, base_y, shadow_radii=None, ground=False)`. Ground-level items (flowers,
  lily pads, the greenhouse footing, the pier, puddles) draw first; then shadows; then
  everything else sorted by base y, characters included. Anything flat that people stand on
  must be `ground=True` or it draws over them.
- `character(name, x, y)` places a rig character's standing frame; `trees(spots)` and
  `tree_line(side, gaps, depth, seed, step, span, spruce)` fill a forest edge.
- `save(VIEWS)` writes the renders and 13×8-tile phone crops and asserts every pixel is DB16.
- **To add an area**, copy any area script (`fold.py` is the smallest), keep the shape:
  `build()` returns the Area, `VIEWS` names the phone crops, `build_all.py` lists the module.

**`props.py`, `creatures.py`, `crops.py`, `people.py`:** every sprite outside the Homestead's
original set, grouped by area in `props.py` (gates first). Helpers: `shaded_ellipse`,
`leaf_blobs` (overlapping lit bundles, used by every bush, hedge, bramble and the oak),
`planks`, `stones`, `rock_face` (uneven strata with cracks; `stones` alone read as brickwork).
Animals are side views facing right, mirrored by `facing_left`. `crops.crop(name, stage)`
draws carrot, potato and radish at sprout/growing/ready. `people.py` only lists the cast:
every person is a rig character, placed with `Area.character(name, x, y)`.

**Animation.** `kit.FRAMES` is 4. `water` and `stream` are animated materials (the
highlight dashes slide one pixel a frame; `overlay_tile` caches per frame). An animated
sprite is a list of `(Sprite, anchor)` frames (`props.waterfall`, `smoke`, `campfire_lit`);
`Area.add` takes the list and `render(frame)` picks the frame. `Area.save(views, animated)`
writes a 4-frame GIF for each view named in `animated`. Streams are one-vertex lines
(`Area.line(..., width=1)`), kept 4-connected so they never pinch on a diagonal.

**Characters beyond Ray and the farmer** are in `characters/rig/wardrobe.py`: `HATS` (rows
0-4 plus a `rise` above head_top) composed with `FACE`, `WHOLE` heads for helmets and hoods,
and `TORSOS` (shirt, apron, coat, vest, robe; the rig's own overalls stay in rig.py). New
roles `A a` are a second garment or hat accent. `rig.py` merges these into its `HEADS`,
`TORSOS` and `CHARACTERS`, so `build.sh` builds all 14. `lineup.py` writes
`build/lineup.png` (everyone at 4× in five poses); look at it after every change.

**Area decisions (settled):**
- The farmyard is a 2-tile road with small aprons at each door plus clutter. One big path
  rectangle read as dead space.
- Path color: DB16 tan alone reads pink and olive-grey reads as asphalt, so it's a tan base
  with a brown `N` rim and olive grit. Tested side by side.
- Small starting area on purpose: Stardew's 94%-tillable Standard Farm is the one players call
  a blank canvas, and the loved farms are 49–64% tillable. The big field is the Old Fields,
  its own area.
- Cobbles at 4× were glittery with a glint on every third stone; now 6% of stones glint.
- The Fold's south tree line is one deep, or the trees cover the pen's bottom fence. Check
  every fence and every standing person against the tree lines after moving anything.
- The Oak's south trees are two deep for the same reason (Skye and Bea vanished behind three).

## The review loop that worked

1. Generate.
2. Look at the phone-scale crops or contact sheets, not the whole map at thumbnail size.
3. Name the specific problem (arm too long, halo bow, pink path), fix that one thing, and
   regenerate.
4. Anything Kayo has approved gets a regression check before more changes land on top of it.
5. Show Kayo a rendered page (artifact), never a schematic of labeled boxes. Earlier proposals
   drawn as rectangles were rejected on sight.

## Environment traps

- **Worktree safety guard:** in a worktree-isolated Claude session, Bash refuses inline
  heredoc Python, pipelines feeding stdin to unknown programs, and commands built from shell
  variables. Write a script file and run `python3 /abs/path/script.py`.
- `cd` doesn't persist between Bash calls, so use absolute paths.
- Pillow 12.3: `Image.getdata()` is deprecated; use `get_flattened_data()`.
